Why something like ClutterDock is needed

The Dock was designed for a handful of favorites — not modern multi-tool workflows.

Most people don’t open software randomly. They open contexts: client work, creative tools, admin utilities, personal apps, research tabs. The system Dock (and Windows taskbar/tray) only gives you a single flat row of icons. So you either:

  • Pin everything — and the Dock becomes a wall of tiny icons you scan every time.
  • Pin almost nothing — and you lose time in Spotlight, Start, or Finder every switch.
  • Scatter launchers — desktop aliases, browser bookmarks, sticky notes with paths.

That friction is small once and huge over a year. ClutterDock exists because launch clutter is a real productivity tax, and the built-in Dock doesn’t give you folders of mixed things (apps + files + folders + URLs) without hacks or replacing the Dock entirely.

Dock space is finite

Every extra icon costs attention. When half your Dock is “maybe later” apps, finding the one you need right now gets slower — especially on smaller screens.

Work isn’t only apps

You also open project folders, PDFs, spreadsheets, and site links. Those don’t belong as permanent Dock icons, but they belong next to the apps you use with them.

Contexts change all day

Morning admin, afternoon design, evening personal. Flat Dock lists don’t match how you actually switch modes — and full Dock replacements are heavy, fragile, or overkill.

Privacy & simplicity matter

Many “productivity” tools want accounts and cloud sync first. ClutterDock is local-first: your stacks stay on your machine. No account required to organize your day.

How ClutterDock helps

Less hunting. Clearer contexts. Same Dock you already know.

1. Collapse many icons into a few stacks

Put “Work,” “Creative,” “Utilities,” or “Client A” into folders. Your Dock can stay short — one ClutterDock icon (or menu bar / tray access) instead of twenty rarely used pins.

2. Mix apps with the files and links they need

A stack isn’t just apps. Drop a project folder, a brief, and the site you open every morning next to the tools you use for that work. One place per context.

3. Launch without leaving the keyboard

Global hotkey (default ⌘⇧D on Mac, Ctrl+Shift+D on Windows). Arrow keys, quick search in a folder, and optional Pro tools like search-across-folders and per-folder hotkeys for power users.

4. Keep the system Dock intact

ClutterDock does not replace or hack the Dock. Public APIs only. That means more stability, fewer “broke after OS update” surprises, and no learning a completely new desktop shell.

5. Smart lists for momentum

Recents tracks what you opened from ClutterDock. Running shows what’s live now. Less manual tidy-up when you’re already mid-flow.

6. Optional power without a subscription

Free forever for daily stacks (with fair limits). Pro is a planned one-time unlock for unlimited folders, workspaces, themes, and more — not a monthly tax on organizing your Dock.

What ClutterDock is not

Clear boundaries help you know if it fits.

Not a Dock replacement

We don’t hide or re-skin the system Dock. ClutterDock is a launcher you open when you need stacks.

Not a full Spotlight rewrite

System search stays great for “open anything.” ClutterDock is for your curated sets — the things you open on purpose, often.

Not cloud-first

No account required. Stacks live on your device. Optional cloud sync may come later as a separate choice.

Free core. One-time Pro.

Start with enough folders and items for daily use. Unlock unlimited stacks, workspaces, and power tools once — not every month.
